03 · Timeline

Slot by slot.

1
09:30
Depart Ikebukuro · JR Shōnan-Shinjuku Line direct to Kamakura (~60 min, no transfer, ¥950) — also stops at Shinjuku & Shibuya. The JR Yokosuka Line from Tokyo Station is equivalent (~60 min). Transfer at Kamakura Station to the Enoden for Hase (~5 min, 4 stops). Buy a 1-day Enoden pass if you'll loop the coast. 05 · Don't miss

Visual references.

Kotoku-in
The Great Buddha of Kamakura
Cast 1252. The temple roof that used to cover it washed away in 1495. Step inside for ¥50 — hollow, oddly intimate.
Hase Station (Enoden)
Tiny coastal line
The Enoden runs through people's gardens at points. 4-car trains, ¥260 single. Buy the 1-day pass if you'll do Hase + Inamuragasaki.
Hase Station → Akatsuki 3-minute walk, easy to find
  1. Exit Hase Station (Enoden) — only one exit, on the south side
  2. Turn right onto the main road heading toward Kotoku-in
  3. Walk ~100m — Akatsuki is on the left, small sign-only entrance
  4. Arrive by 10:55 — reservation 11:00 sharp
Kamakura Station transfer Switching to the Enoden
  1. Get off the JR train at Kamakura Station (both Shōnan-Shinjuku & Yokosuka lines terminate here)
  2. Exit the JR ticket gates, walk through the small square
  3. Enoden gates are immediately to the left — buy a 1-day pass from the machine (¥800)
  4. 4 stops to Hase — about 5 min, single platform
06 · Tips for today

Worth remembering.

Akatsuki 11:00 sharp. Don't be late. Aim to be on the 09:30 Shōnan-Shinjuku from Ikebukuro. If JR is delayed, message ahead via the booking system. Worst case, take a taxi from Kamakura Stn to Hase (¥1,500, 8 min).
Komachi-dori snacking strategy. Skip the food after Akatsuki. Just window shop, grab a Toshimaya box, head back.
Fuji from Shichirigahama. Clear days only. Sunset alignment is best September–March; in May the angle is off, but still a nice silhouette.
